TXIT Exam Guide: What to Expect on Test Day

Ready to ace the Radiation Oncology In-Training Exam (TXIT)? Here is what to expect on your test day.

The TXIT is administered annually to radiation oncology residents to assess knowledge and progress, and to compare performance across all residency levels. Your exam results will be provided to your Program Director in approximately 8 weeks. Before you sit down for the in-service exam, review the technical requirements and instructions below carefully.

System & Technical Requirements

To take the TXIT, you must have an active ACR Member User Account, using the primary email address created to log in to ACR.org.

Make sure your setup meets these specifications:

  • Operating System: Windows 10 or higher for PC; macOS High Sierra or higher for Mac.
  • Processor & RAM: A dual-core 2.4 GHz CPU with 8 GB RAM or better for PC; an Intel or AMD processor with 8 GB RAM for Mac.
  • Web Browser: The latest version of Google Chrome (preferred) or Mozilla Firefox. Safari is NOT supported.
  • Browser Settings: JavaScript must be enabled, and third-party cookies must be accepted.
  • Display & Connection: A monitor with a camera resolution of 800 x 600 or better, a keyboard, a mouse, and a stable, high-speed connection (DSL or better).

Time, Environment, and Allowed Items for the In-Training Exam

You will have 3 hours to complete the TXIT. Here's what's allowed and what isn't.

Allowed items:

  • Food and beverages during the exam.
  • A standard battery- or solar-powered calculator (not part of a cellular device), or the built-in Windows/macOS calculator on screen.
  • On-screen note-taking using MS Word, Notepad, or TextEdit.

Prohibited items:

  • Cell phones and any internet-enabled devices (iPads, iPod Touch, etc).
  • Headphones or earbuds.
  • Any external digital resource.

Breaks & Confirmation

You're permitted one ten-minute break during the 3-hour exam, but the clock keeps running during your break. Take it whenever you like. When you step away, leave your computer as-is, don't turn it off.

Before starting, you'll see this on-screen notice:

"This is a timed exam scheduled for 3 hours. Once started, the clock will continue counting without stopping, even if you log out of the system. Please confirm to continue."

You must click Confirm to acknowledge the exam duration is three hours and must be completed in one sitting.

How to Prepare for the RadOnc In-Service Exam

Knowing the exam logistics is only half the battle. The TXIT covers 13 core domains, statistics, bone & soft tissue, breast, central nervous system (CNS) & eye, gastrointestinal, genitourinary, gynecology, head & neck & skin, lung & mediastinum, lymphoma & leukemia, pediatrics, biology, and physics, so broad, consistent review across categories matters more than last-minute cramming.

Build familiarity with high-yield questions and evidence-based rationales across all of these domains before test day.
